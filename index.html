How I work

Metrics first,
pixels second.

My data background means I don't open Figma until I know what "better" looks like and how we'll measure it.

Frame

Turn a vague ask into one clear problem, with a north-star metric and guardrails agreed before any design.

Problem statement · HMW · metric tree

Listen

Talk to real learners, read support tickets, and dig into funnels to find where people actually drop off.

Interviews · SQL · journey maps

Make

Go wide in sketches, narrow in Figma, then build a coded prototype so it feels real, latency and all.

Figma · Claude Code · Cursor

Test

Five users, real tasks, one question: where do they hesitate? Fix that, then test again.

Task-based tests · SUS · think-aloud

Measure

Ship with tracking built in. Read the data after launch, and keep improving.

Event specs · dashboards · A/B
AI in my design work

A faster loop,
not a shortcut.

I use AI at every step of my process. The judgement stays mine.

My rule
AI drafts.
I decide.
Users verify.

AI makes it cheap to try ten directions. It doesn't know which one is right. Only research and testing can tell me that.

About

The hardest part of an AI system is the moment a person has to trust it.

I'm an M.Tech Data Science student at IIT Roorkee. Before that I spent a year at Forage AI turning messy web-scraped data into something people could rely on, and I published two machine learning papers during my undergrad at SRMIST.

Somewhere between building models and coordinating campus placements, I noticed that the hardest problems usually weren't in the model. They were in the moment a person has to understand, trust and act on what the system tells them. That's a design problem. So now I do both: I use data to find the problem and design to solve it for people.

I think in Hindi, Marathi and English, and I'm happiest when I'm close to the learners I'm designing for.
