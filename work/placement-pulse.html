Placement Pulse: making the placement journey calm

As a Placement Coordinator at IIT Roorkee, I sit between companies and students. From that seat I watched capable students miss opportunities because the process wasn't clear, not because they lacked skill. So I mapped the journey end to end and designed one place that always tells a student what's next.

Concept grounded in lived experience. This isn't an official IIT Roorkee project. The observations are my own, from coordinating drives. The screens are hi-fi concepts, the metrics are proposals, and no real student data was used.
RoleResearcher & product designer
TimelineOct – Nov 2026
PlatformMobile (students) · Web (coordinators)
ToolsFigma · FigJam · Claude Code
Problem

Updates are scattered across email, WhatsApp and the portal. Students miss deadlines, and coordinators end up working as human reminder services.

Solution

A "Next up" timeline for every student, eligibility at a glance, one-tap registration, and a coordinator board that catches clashes before they happen.

Output

A journey map, problem framing with success metrics, three key flows as hi-fi screens, and a decision log.

01 · Context

I've seen it from the inside.

As coordinator I work with corporate HR teams to schedule interviews and run drive logistics. That puts me where every confused question ends up. The pattern repeated drive after drive:

What I saw

The same questions, over and over

"Am I eligible?" "Is the test online?" "Which CV do I upload?" "What's the deadline?"

What I saw

Three sources, none in sync

Official email, WhatsApp groups and the portal often said slightly different things.

What I saw

Avoidable misses

Late registrations, the wrong CV version, a missed test link, all with real consequences.

What I saw

Interview-day chaos

Slot clashes between companies, and students stuck in two queues at once.

None of these are skill problems. They're information design problems.

How I gathered insight

  • My coordinator notes: a running list of recurring questions and failure points during drives.
  • Informal conversations with batchmates going through placements and with fellow coordinators.
  • A tool audit of the current setup: email, portal, WhatsApp groups and shared spreadsheets, and what each one is actually used for.
02 · Insights

Four things that changed my brief.

Insight 1

Students don't need more information. They need the next step.

Every message was another thing to read. Nobody told them what to do.

Insight 2

Eligibility anxiety blocks action

Unsure whether they qualify, some students simply don't apply, or apply everywhere and clog the process.

Insight 3

Trust follows the source

When the portal and WhatsApp disagree, students trust whichever forward reached them first. One source of truth beats more channels.

Insight 4

Coordinators are reminder systems

Most coordinator time goes into chasing confirmations, not into coordination that needs a human.

04 · Framing & metrics

One question to design for.

How might we make sure every student always knows their next step, so coordinators can stop being reminder services?

MetricTypeWhy it matters
On-time registrationNorth star% of eligible students who register before the deadline. This directly measures "no one misses out."
Time to registerInputHours from drive announced to registration. Faster means clearer.
Repeat queries per driveInput"Am I eligible?" and "When's the test?" messages logged by coordinators. Should fall sharply.
Interview slot clashesInputClashes caught after the schedule is published. Target: zero.
Notification opt-outsGuardrailIf we fix missed deadlines by spamming, students will mute us and we're back to square one.
Coordinator hours / driveBusinessTime freed for the work that needs a human, like talking to recruiters.
05 · Solution

Three flows, one source of truth.

A. "Next up": the home screen answers one question

Students open the app to find out what to do, so the home screen says it directly: what's due, how long is left, and a single button to do it.

B. Eligibility at a glance, with dead ends turned into redirects

Students shouldn't have to read a PDF to find out whether they qualify. The check runs against their profile and shows each criterion. If they don't qualify, it says why and suggests drives they can apply for.

C. Coordinator board: catch clashes before students do

On interview day, coordinators need to see every room and every student at once. The board flags a student booked in two places and lets the coordinator fix it before anyone's stuck in two queues.

06 · Tradeoffs

The decision log

✕ Considered

Replace WhatsApp entirely

One clean channel, full control.

✓ Chose

App as source of truth, WhatsApp for delivery

Official messages link back to the app.

Why: students live on WhatsApp, and fighting that habit is a fight the product loses. Insight 3 said the real problem was conflicting sources, not the number of channels. So every message points to a single page that's always correct.
✕ Considered

Always-on countdown timers

Maximum urgency, fewer misses.

✓ Chose

Countdown only in the final 24h

Calm dates before that.

Why: placement season is already stressful. Constant red timers would cut misses but raise anxiety and opt-outs. The notification opt-out guardrail is there to keep this decision honest.
✕ Considered

Hide drives you're not eligible for

A cleaner feed.

✓ Chose

Show them, with reasons and redirects

Transparency over tidiness.

Why: a missing drive looks like a bug or a rumour ("I heard X is coming?"). Showing why builds trust in the system and turns a dead end into a next step.
07 · Learnings & next steps

The best feature was the one I removed.

My first draft had a feed, a chat, a calendar and a prep forum. Mapping the journey showed that students didn't lack features. They lacked a clear next step. Cutting back to "Next up" made everything else make sense.

This applies well beyond one campus. Any platform that promises learning that ends in a job runs this same journey: applications, tests, mock interviews, offers. The principles carry over directly: one source of truth, eligibility upfront, and always showing the next step.

What I'd do next

  • Usability test the registration and eligibility flows with 5 students before the next drive season.
  • Pilot with a single company drive and compare on-time registration with the previous drive.
  • Prep kits: design how seniors contribute interview notes without exposing confidential questions.
