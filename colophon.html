01 · The brief I gave myself

Start from the reader, not the résumé.

I began with the job description, not my CV. I broke every line of it into a checklist, then asked an uncomfortable question: where on my site would a recruiter find proof of each one?

The honest answer exposed three gaps: I haven't held a formal design job, I needed two or three case studies that show my process, and I needed a real vibe-coded project. So the strategy became:

  • Make my data background the differentiator. Most junior designers can't write SQL or explain why a RAG system fails. I can, so every case study leads with metrics and tradeoffs.
  • Use lived experience as research. I'm a Placement Coordinator, so I've seen placement journeys up close. That became a case study.
  • Show, don't claim. A working prototype beats a paragraph about prototyping. A "show my thinking" toggle beats a paragraph about process.
  • Be honest about what's a concept. Every case study says clearly what was shipped and what's a concept.
02 · Process

Seven steps, one evening at a time.

1 · Decode

Turned the JD into a requirements checklist (it's on the home page).

2 · Inventory

Listed my real work and matched each piece to a requirement.

3 · Direction

Picked a visual concept: an editorial serif plus mono "redline" annotations, a designer who shows the reasoning.

4 · Build

Prompted Claude Code page by page, reviewing every diff and steering layout, copy and interactions.

5 · Prototype

Built Ask Newton as a scripted prototype with real states, timing and analytics events.

6 · Check

Tested on phone widths, in dark mode, with the keyboard only, and with reduced motion.

7 · Ship

Pushed to GitHub and deployed as a static site. No server needed.

Components

Buttons, chips, tags, cards, decision logs, metrics tables, annotated mocks, phone frames, a journey map and the TOC are all built once and reused across every case study. Each new page costs less than the last, which is the whole point of a design system.

04 · Constraints

Rules that kept it fast and honest.

No framework

Plain HTML, CSS and JS. It loads fast, hosts anywhere for free, and I can read every line.

Accessible by default

Semantic HTML, keyboard focus states, a skip link, sufficient contrast, and reduced motion respected.

Phone-first checks

Every page works at 375px wide. Recruiters often open links on their phones.

No fake numbers

Metrics in concept case studies are proposals, never invented results.
